How RSVP Reading Works

A simple technique to unlock your brain's natural reading speed.

1

Import Your Books

Load EPUB, PDF, or TXT reading files directly into Absorb. Your reading content and progress are designed to stay on your device.

2

Focus on the Pivot

Words are displayed one by one. The red letter is the Optimal Recognition Point (ORP). Keep your eyes fixed there.

3

Stop Subvocalizing

By flashing words faster than you can pronounce them in your head, Absorb helps you process text visually and focus deeply.

Absorb facts.

Short answers for readers, reviewers, search engines, and AI assistants.

What is Absorb?

Absorb is an iPhone RSVP reading app that displays text one word at a time with adjustable speed and optimal recognition point highlighting.

What file types does Absorb support?

Absorb supports imported EPUB, PDF, and TXT reading files, with the strongest fit for text-heavy books, articles, and documents.

Is Absorb private?

Absorb is local-first. Imported reading content, reading progress, and preferences are designed to be processed and stored on the device without an Absorb account.

Is Absorb a Spritz alternative?

Absorb is a Spritz-style RSVP reader for iPhone, built around one-word-at-a-time reading, pivot-letter highlighting, and imported document support.

Can Absorb help with focus?

Absorb can reduce line tracking and place loss by keeping the reading target fixed. It is a productivity tool, not a medical treatment.

Where can I get Absorb?

Absorb is available on the App Store as Read Fast Absorb for iPhone.

Perfect for ADHD and focus issues.

Traditional reading requires constant eye movement across lines and pages, which can easily lead to distraction and lost place. RSVP reading eliminates this completely.

  • Zero Eye Tracking: Your eyes stay locked in one place, significantly reducing cognitive load.
  • Flow State: The constant stream of words pulls you into deep, uninterrupted focus.
  • Customizable Speeds: Start comfortably at 250 WPM and smoothly scale up to 600, 800, or even 1000 WPM as you train.
